# Building a signed template library (non-normative guide)

Part of the Three Senses Alerting Standard. This guide binds no conformance requirement.
It describes how an alerting authority, with its national Deaf community, can build the
library of signed clips that rules S2 and S3 of THE-STANDARD.md and section 2
of CAP-PROFILE.md rely on.

A signed clip in such a library explains what an alert TYPE means. It is not a translation
of one specific message; the text channels carry the agency, the place and the time.

## The path

1. A small vocabulary first. Start with the hazard families and the all-clear, about
   twenty clips. A short list that is finished is worth more than a long list that is not.
2. A Deaf signer who is native in the national sign language, and where possible a Deaf
   interpreter rather than a hearing one.
3. Review by the national Deaf association before any encoding. Review after encoding
   means refilming.
4. The alerting authority approves each clip as its own message, the way it approves the
   written template. An unapproved clip is not an alert.
5. Clips are issued to receivers in advance and verified by the CAP resource `digest`, so
   that a television plays a verified clip from its own storage, with the network down.
   The live alert names the clip; the receiver plays its stored copy when the digest
   matches and fetches only when it does not.
6. Every clip is versioned, and is approved again whenever the template text changes.

The expensive part is the approval loop, not the filming. Start it first.

## Why the relay does not fix hard words

A relay shows the authority's text unchanged (rule S3). If the authority's wording is
not simple enough, the remedy is a better template, approved upstream, and then a new
clip. It is never an edit downstream.

## Precedent

The Federal Communications Commission's 2025 order adopted eighteen Wireless Emergency
Alert templates in thirteen written languages and in American Sign Language. The ASL
versions were produced by FEMA with Deaf signers, and device support is due by June 2028.
The pairing of those templates with their videos is in `wea-asl-templates.json`.
International Deaf Emergency knows of no authority-approved library of signed clips per
hazard outside the United States as of October 2026. A reader who knows of one is asked to
say so.
